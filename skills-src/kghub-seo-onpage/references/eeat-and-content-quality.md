# E-E-A-T & Content Quality Framework

Condensed from claude-seo's `seo-content` skill (MIT licensed,
github.com/AgriciDaniel/claude-seo), keeping only what's actionable when
editing an already-written article's text directly.

## E-E-A-T Framework (per Google's Sept 2025 Quality Rater Guidelines)

### Experience (first-hand signals)
- Original research, case studies, before/after results
- Personal anecdotes, process documentation
- Unique data, proprietary insights
- Specificity that reads as first-hand rather than generic

### Expertise
- Technical depth appropriate for the audience
- Accurate, well-sourced claims
- Confident, specific language rather than hedged generalities

### Authoritativeness
- References/citations to other authoritative sources where relevant
- Claims that would hold up if fact-checked

### Trustworthiness (Google has stated this is the most heavily weighted of the four)
- Transparent, accurate claims — never invented facts
- Clear dates/timeliness where relevant
- Internal consistency (no contradicting an earlier part of the same article)

## Content Metrics

### Readability
- Flesch Reading Ease: a useful proxy for accessibility (target ~60-70 for
  a general audience), but NOT a direct Google ranking factor — use it as a
  content-quality indicator, not an SEO metric to chase.
- Sentence length: average 15-20 words.
- Paragraph length: 2-4 sentences.

### Keyword Optimization
- Primary keyword (only if one is given) in title, H1, and first ~100 words.
- Natural density (1-3%), semantic variations present, no stuffing.

### Content Structure
- Logical heading hierarchy (H1 -> H2 -> H3).
- Scannable sections with descriptive (not decorative) headings.
- Bullet/numbered lists where the content is naturally list-shaped
  (steps, comparisons) — don't force it where prose reads better.

## AI Content Assessment

Google's raters assess low-quality, scaled, or generic-AI-pattern content —
not "was AI involved" as a standalone issue. Acceptable AI-assisted content
demonstrates genuine E-E-A-T, unique value, and reads as edited/considered
rather than templated. Low-quality markers to fix when editing: generic
phrasing with no specificity, no original insight, repetitive structure,
factual looseness.

> **Gen-AI optimization is SEO** (Google's official position, 2026-06-29
> guide): no new AI-specific files, markup, content chunking, or
> AI-rephrasing are needed. Chasing inauthentic "mentions" is unhelpful.
> AEO/GEO is rebranded SEO rooted in the same core ranking/quality signals.

> **Honest scoping**: no tool (including this one) has access to Google's
> internal ranking signals. Treat this framework as an editorial heuristic
> for writing genuinely better content, not a guarantee of ranking impact.
